# Proposed v13: vector action-response critic

Status: design only; no trainer implemented or run. This follows the stopped v12 long-run experiment. It preserves fresh runs, no replay, full-batch fitting at 16 × 2,048 transitions, no GAE, no repeated Bellman sweeps, and a KL ceiling of 0.03. Only this thread's methods and results informed the design. Two independent mathematical/design reviews were used.

## Diagnosis and decision

Increased Beta concentration is not itself evidence of exploration collapse. The v12 logs do not establish that exploration caused the performance plateau. Nor do they prove objective mismatch caused it; it is the leading design concern.

V12 predicts physical state transitions with coordinate-normalized MSE, then consumes their derivatives to generate reward credit. Small value error does not control derivative error. Weighting transition residuals by the costate improves relevance but does not solve this: a perturbation with small amplitude and rapidly varying action dependence can preserve small prediction error while producing large derivative error. The latent costate also learns targets generated by that model, so apparent self-consistency need not mean correct environmental credit.

Recommendation: replace that path with a vector prediction of **how the current action changes the future reward profile under the collection policy**. Compute expected changes under candidate Beta policies analytically. Do not differentiate the learned physical transition map.

This is an action-value formulation with a structured vector output. Its justification is a more direct relationship between prediction error and policy-gradient error, not the output dimension by itself. Any return-maximizing actor eventually contracts information into a scalar objective. The requirement is to avoid compressing the critic's learning targets and intermediate credit into a single scalar advantage.

## What the critic predicts

For each real state and episode age, predict:

1. A vector of state-dependent mean future reward components.
2. A tensor of action-response coefficients for those components.
3. Learned action-response basis parameters that permit analytic Beta integration.

For HalfCheetah the observable components are forward progress and each actuator's control expenditure, separated over nonoverlapping future time intervals. Each target is a different observed quantity, not another copy of the scalar total return. Control weights and signs come from the environment; summing the signed components over all intervals exactly recovers the observed reward-to-go. There must be no independently chosen head weighting or discount at actor contraction that silently changes the reward objective.

The mean profile is an intercept for separating predictable state-dependent outcomes from action effects. Give this nuisance predictor separate parameters from the action-response feature network, so fitting easy state-dependent levels does not consume that network's representation learning directly. Both are trained against the same observed profile through their summed prediction. The intercept is a vector, and cancels from the actor's expected-improvement objective. There is no scalar V/Q prediction used as the actor advantage or as a bootstrap bottleneck.

With component index c and basis index k:

    predicted_profile_c(s,a) = baseline_profile_c(s)
                              + sum_k coefficients_ck(s) * features_k(s,a)

The coefficient tensor and basis parameters are the critic's latent output. Profile reconstruction supervises actual collected state/action pairs. No predicted next state is decoded, and no learned state decoder is evaluated on generated states.

Distinct profile targets make the supervision vector-valued; they do not prove that this representation will outperform a scalar Q critic. That must be earned by end-to-end return.

## Action representation and Beta integration

Include the exact centered, Fisher-whitened old-policy Beta log-score coordinates for every actuator. These provide the complete local alpha/beta score directions, including concentration changes. A mean-action-only basis cannot provide that guarantee.

Augment them with learned integrable action-response kernels. One available family, for native actions x in (0,1), is:

    kernel_k(x) = product_i x_i**p_ki * (1-x_i)**q_ki

For nonnegative exponents:

    E_Beta[kernel_k] = product_i B(alpha_i+p_ki, beta_i+q_ki)
                                  / B(alpha_i, beta_i)

The exponents can depend on the observed state, but are held fixed during actor optimization. Begin with broad single-actuator and actuator-pair interactions; arbitrarily narrow joint-action kernels are poorly identified by fresh samples. The exact score-coordinate expectations also have analytic digamma expressions. Stable evaluation should use log-beta functions, with numerical derivative contracts against Monte Carlo/autograd references through mlq before training.

Center features using the collection policy. Freeze that centering throughout each actor update. Re-centering at the candidate policy would make the expected centered features identically zero and destroy the intended improvement calculation.

Kernel shapes, coefficient tensors, and profile intercepts are frozen while optimizing the actor. Updating the actor must not differentiate through the critic's state-dependent predictions or through a candidate-policy redefinition of the feature family.

## Targets and full-batch fitting

The initial formulation uses actual future component sums to an observed episode end. Targets are fixed through fitting. There is no GAE or Bellman iteration, and no invented zero tail for unfinished episodes.

A transition at the start of a rollout can be used even if its episode began under an earlier policy: its remaining actions are generated by the current fixed collection policy. We need a complete *future suffix*, not necessarily an episode that begins inside this rollout. Right-censored suffixes at the rollout boundary lack a complete target and are masked out of critic regression. The actor can still use all rollout states with known age, relying on the learned response model there.

For a 2,048-step rollout and staggered 1,000-step episodes, this usually retains approximately three quarters of transitions for Monte Carlo profile fitting. The actual fraction must be logged. Changing the policy before collecting a target suffix would change its meaning; retaining an old suffix and silently completing it after an actor update is not acceptable. No historical replay is introduced.

Fit the whole eligible batch on the joint profile reconstruction loss. Learn baseline, response coefficients, and kernel shapes together. Use profile squared error in actual reward units initially. If outputs are numerically rescaled, restore both predictions and the squared loss weights to preserve that objective. Inverting the outputs alone does not undo the change in fitting priorities from normalizing each head's loss.

Shared latent factors can reduce the coefficient tensor's cost, but they limit conditional expressivity. Architecture ranks and temporal resolution remain implementation choices, not demonstrated optima. The method needs to represent actuator interactions and concentration effects, not just six independent mean shifts.

## Actor objective

For a candidate policy, compute the expected reward-profile change exactly under the fitted response model:

    delta_profile(s) = coefficients(s) *
                      [E_candidate(features) - E_collection(features)]

Only then sum the true signed reward components and average over the fresh rollout states. Optimize that scalar improvement subject to exact mean Beta KL <= 0.03.

Use the nonlinear expected response for candidate acceptance. Keeping v12's frozen first-order alpha/beta surrogate would discard the response model's curvature information. The KL is a ceiling: when the fitted response has an interior optimum or predicts no useful gain, there is no reason to force a boundary-sized move. A first implementation can search its natural-gradient ray using this nonlinear objective; that is a restricted optimization, not a global constrained optimum. Further constrained optimization is an implementation tradeoff to evaluate honestly.

The response is Q under the collection policy: it describes changing the current action while retaining that future policy. It is not an exact prediction of return after changing the policy everywhere. State-occupancy shift, approximation error, and unsupported action regions remain limitations of local policy improvement.

## Why this objective is better aligned

Fix a state and collection policy. Let z be its whitened action score, with E[z]=0 and E[z z^T]=I. Let e(a) be error in the critic's summed conditional return prediction. The error in the corresponding expected policy gradient, measured in whitened policy coordinates, is:

    E[e(a) * z(a)]

Orthogonal projection gives the bound:

    squared_norm(E[e*z]) <= E[e**2]

Thus action-value prediction error controls the *expected* policy-gradient error in this metric. It does not need to control an arbitrary pointwise derivative of a learned physics model. The same statement does not automatically bound Euclidean actor-parameter error; actor Jacobians and shared parameterization still matter.

For vector profile errors e_vec and true reward weights w, the relevant summed error is w^T e_vec. Its mean square includes correlations between components. Low individual head errors are not independent evidence, and one must not ignore coherent errors when judging the actor objective. Full-vector MSE supplies a bound via the norm of w, rather than equality with the return-gradient loss.

There is also a precise population target: if every exact score coordinate is included and its per-state coefficients are freely adjustable, unregularized conditional least squares makes each component's residual orthogonal to the score. It then reproduces the true local alpha/beta gradient even when higher-order response shape remains imperfect. A finite shared neural network trained for a few epochs does not automatically satisfy those normal equations.

These identities concern conditional mean prediction error under the collection distribution. Observed Monte Carlo profile loss also contains trajectory noise; a small training loss does not establish a small population conditional prediction error. The identities are not a guarantee of correctness at a new policy, of optimal environment behavior, or of successful finite-data learning.

## Difference from v9, and alternatives rejected

V9 directly regressed noisy products of future reward and policy score onto state. The new formulation learns the conditional action-response profile, separates state-dependent mean outcomes, and integrates a learned nonlinear response over candidate policies. This can reduce nuisance variation and model finite policy changes, but its correct first-order population gradient is the same quantity v9 targeted. A fixed linear-score-only implementation would be too close to that failed approach to justify a new claim.

Merely adding more heads, repeating scalar return labels, using only quadratic action moments, or renaming a scalar Q decoder would not establish the desired benefit. Learned interactions, meaningful component targets, exact integration, and nonlinear actor evaluation are substantive parts of the proposal.

Directly training the whole vector update operator on the return of the next policy is even more literally aligned. Its outer gradient, however, depends on a noisy fresh policy-gradient estimate and differentiation through the actor update. Hard line-search decisions complicate that derivative, and one update is not a whole learning trajectory. It is a credible later research direction, but not an obviously better first replacement for a critic with identifiable prediction targets.

## Evaluation decision

If implemented, use a new single-file version, fresh seed 1, the same 32,768-transition rollout, full-batch fitting, no replay, no GAE, and no inner Bellman target sweeps. Test the analytic integration, alpha/beta derivatives, centering, profile-to-reward identity, and episode censoring numerically through mlq. Then judge a fresh 8M HalfCheetah run by achieved training return and its learning curve. No held-out promotion gate and no automatic long-run extension.

This design removes the identified dependence on inaccurate learned physics derivatives. It does not establish that the remaining response prediction problem is easy, that vector supervision is superior, or that the design is optimal. No implementation or training has been launched from this document.
