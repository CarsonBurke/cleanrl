# Independent numerical derivation for the layer Fisher V2 repair

It does not relax the original1e-13 uniform requirement, execute a model/test, or alter frozen V1. Implementation approval and CUDA execution remain separate.

[NIST DLMF5.15](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.15) supplies trigamma series T(x)=sum_k>=0 1/(x+k)^2, recurrence T(x)=T(x+1)+x^-2, and expansion T(x)~1/x+1/(2x²)+sum_n>=1 B(2n)/x^(2n+1). The finite remainder argument below is independently derived; it does not infer a differentiated error bound from a log-Gamma bound.

For real x>0, integrating the positive series gives T(x)=integral_0^infinity [t/(1-exp(-t))] exp(-xt) dt. The coth partial-fraction identity gives t/(1-exp(-t))=1+t/2+2t² sum_n>=1 1/(t²+(2*pi*n)²). Expand each reciprocal in t² through seven terms using the finite geometric identity, retaining its exact signed remainder. The integrated included coefficients are B2/x³ through B14/x15, with B14=7/6. The omitted remainder is negative: if S7 is that finite expansion, 0<=S7(x)-T(x)<=|B16|/x17, where |B16|=3617/510. Positivity allows termwise integration and the first-omitted bound for every real x>0; no asymptotic-only heuristic is needed.

Shift both arguments by SAME fixed N=32. Recurrence is exact over real arithmetic, and the maximum standalone omitted error is (3617/510)/33^17 for shapes>=1, comfortably below double roundoff at that argument. For shape Fisher diagonal D(a,b)=T(a)-T(a+b), do not subtract two already-rounded nearly equal trigamma results when a>>b.

At each recurrence index j, let v=a+j,w=a+b+j,u=1/v,z=1/w and delta=(b/w)*u. Then u-z=delta mathematically, without subtracting nearly equal reciprocals. The reciprocal-square difference is delta*(u+z). For every asymptotic power p, u^p-z^p=delta*sum_i=0..p-1 u^(p-1-i)*z^i. Apply this factorization to the shifted asymptotic terms with p1,2,3,5,7,9,11,13,15 and their Bernoulli coefficients. Retaining b itself, rather than reconstructing b=(a+b)-a, preserves information even when floating addition loses b entirely.

For the asymptotic diagonal difference the remainder obeys the STRONGER bound:
