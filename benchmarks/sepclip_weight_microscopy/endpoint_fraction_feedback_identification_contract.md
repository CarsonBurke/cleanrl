## Definition required for an exact endpoint-fraction diagnostic

For a specified saved pair theta_old/theta_endpoint, define a FIXED ambient parameter chord. Pin the subtraction, scaling and FP32 rounding order; use all actual actor parameter banks, including projections, residual mixing, gains, biases and constrained head rows. Fraction0 must use the exact saved old bank; fraction1 must use the exact saved endpoint bank directly rather than a potentially rounded subtraction/addition reconstruction. Report any represented interior-bank difference from the intended chord. A fraction of endpoint motion is not the actual first update, an intrinsic optimizer path or fraction of training time.

Do not retract/renormalize/project an interior chord and still label it this same intervention. If an alternative constrained path is considered, it is a separate declared diagnostic whose old-base tangent may differ. Intrinsic legality is not required to evaluate an explicitly labeled ambient diagnostic actor; actual architecture forward and bounds must remain valid. No fractional bank may silently substitute a legacy actor or recover latent logits from rounded shapes.

Use the same restored physical states, own frozen observation/reward rows, horizon, action convention, old-tail labels and actual future actor dispatch for every fraction. Required replay, finite, native-bounds, no-reset and source/checkpoint gates remain those already used for seven-arm replay. Preserve permanent-buffer copying and raw versus clip-then-FP32 reward laws. Keep sampled, mean and actual-shape-mode results separate. Shared inverse-CDF quantiles across every fraction/arm within a replica and independent streams between replicas define the diagnostic coupling; this is not exact native host RNG equivalence.

For each fractionf and each initial state, retain these four returns: old/old, fraction-at-t0/old-later, old-at-t0/fraction-later, fraction/fraction. Let T(f), L(f) and F(f) be their gains from old/old. Define I(f)=F(f)−T(f)−L(f), using the SAME replica/state values. This keeps the finite causal interaction distinct from a score derivative. Its change arises because the first action changes the next state and the new future actor responds differently along that changed trajectory.

## What would identify which claim

1. **Finite local intervention sign at a fixed nonzero fraction:** a resolved paired CI for T(f) or F(f) identifies the sign of that finite conditional intervention under the declared law. This does not require a reliable dense field. One fraction never identifies the derivative at0, even if it is called small.
2. **A numerically supported near-old slope:** examine paired secants T(f)/f for first-action credit, or F(f)/f for full-policy credit, across more than one useful decreasing fraction. Fractional bank/action changes must exceed effective FP32 quantization and simulator precision floors; report represented motion. Require resolved sign and a prespecified accuracy plateau whose uncertainty is small enough to rule out a material slope change. Agreement of noisy point estimates is insufficient. If contacts, action atoms or clipping produce nonsmooth behavior, withhold a differentiable-slope claim even if finite signs resolve. Exact float rounding does not itself supply a smooth mathematical derivative.
3. **Finite response nonlinearity at measured scales:** paired D(f)=G(f)−2G(f/2), where G is T or F, tests deviation from linear scaling between those two intervals. A resolved nonzero D shows finite nonlinearity along that declared path/law. It does not uniquely locate feedback, actor curvature, contact switching or clipping. Under an additional smooth expansion G(f)=f*a+.5*f*f*b+..., D(f)=.25*f*f*b+..., but this expansion must be checked rather than assumed.
4. **Finite feedback coupling:** resolved I(f) identifies the four-arm interaction at that fraction. Under a smooth matched-law expansion it is usually second order in the simultaneous first/later changes; a measured interaction is not automatically a Hessian certificate. To distinguish coupling from first-only physical nonlinearity, retain T,L,F,I at the same fractions. A resolved endpoint I(1) alone does not establish its limiting behavior near0.
5. **Score-estimator agreement:** compare T(f)/f only with the matching first-action smooth-score direction; F(f)/f only with matching all-time credit. Match actual parameter chord, occupancy, normalizer, horizon, reward units and action-law derivative. Existing dense rewards use FP64 normalized clipping whereas physical replay rounds each normalized reward to FP32; this must be explicitly reconciled, not assumed away because no clips were observed. The truncated-quantile/rounded/clipped physical law versus continuous Beta score remains a separate bias limit. A discrepancy with score credit does not identify critic bias. Finite-response signs can be accepted while exact native-law PG remains uncertified.

## Statistical acceptance and what remains unidentified

With R4 fixed-state continuation replicas, compute each signed contrast inside the replica, then average over the32 selected states, then estimate SE across the four replica means. Pointwise approximate95% CI is mean ±3.182446*SE (df3). Curvature and interaction intervals require their own paired arrays; independently summing arm/fraction SEs discards covariance. The32 states increase precision for this fixed empirical target but do not supply32 independent training seeds or certify occupancy generalization. A deterministic fork has no continuation MC uncertainty and still has no state/seed guarantee.

Choose fractions, objectives, horizons, arm contrasts and an effect/precision threshold before inspecting their gains. A useful slope criterion must bound uncertainty relative to the desired slope/error, not simply exclude0. Small fractions may worsen secant SE by1/f; no universal choice of fraction or required replica count follows from existing R4 summaries. Endpoint summary artifacts alone do not contain fraction covariance and cannot predict a near-old confidence interval. Nested horizons are paired and are not independent confirmations.

Multiplicity remains consequential: existing v14 Adam8M endpoint t3 statistic3.580 gives approximate two-sided p=.03728; Anvil4M3.744 gives p=.03325. Both fail even a hypothetical Bonferroni family of only two such tests (critical t3=4.1765). Actual families include more captures/fractions/arms/objectives/modes. For M=6/12/18 pointwise-selected contrasts, Bonferroni t3 thresholds are6.2315/7.9398/9.1294; dependence does not invalidate Bonferroni, but it prevents treating repeated signs as independent evidence. These examples clarify limits, not a retroactive registered family or exact normality certificate.

Current R4 evidence does not identify a unique first-versus-later cause of v14 endpoint signs; a local derivative of the finite sampled physical law; contact or layer mechanisms; true infinite-tail or critic error; native score-law unbiasedness; across-run matched occupancy; or causal benchmark superiority. Old v12 negative local scalar, negative finite t0 effect and unresolved full feedback outcome are mutually compatible. The exact-fraction diagnostic would narrow that ambiguity only if it passes the numerical, paired precision, scale and objective gates above. This is a prospective identification contract, not authorization or recommendation to submit new work.
