The original update mechanism

One rollout collects 16 × 1024 = 16,384 transitions under a fixed host policy. It then performs 16,384 critic/encoder steps with batches of 256: approximately 256 sampled passes. The delayed actor takes 8,192 Adam steps and targets/SALE features change approximately 65 times. No new transition constrains those changes.

The v8 replay actor samples historical states from the same LAP minibatch as the critic. In v9 it samples recent states, but keeps optimizing current-policy critic predictions after the collection distribution has changed. In addition, LAP gives high-TD-error states extra actor weight. “No historical replay” and “on-policy policy gradient” are different properties.

Fresh v9 logs average unclipped-Gaussian KL(behavior || new) around 9,000 late in its observed run. This KL includes scale changes and encoder swaps. Because the environment clips actions, Gaussian KL upper-bounds physical-action KL; it does not mean the executed-action KL is exactly 9,000. It nevertheless demonstrates that behavior-relative Gaussian locality is not maintained.

A mental model of the critic

The network predicts F(s, mu, sigma, u), querying its center at u=mu. Its stochastic regression uses an approximate Hermite reconstruction:

F_center + sum(sigma_i * eps_i * dF/dmu_i)
         + sum(0.5 * sigma_i * (eps_i² - 1) * dF/dsigma_i)
         ≈ reward + gamma * target_value(next_state)

The mean derivative follows all center-query routes, including u and the SALE action embedding. Two additional queries regress the realized pre-action point and executed-action point directly against the TD label.

Hermite terms do supervise derivatives, so “the gradients receive no supervision” would be false. The problem is identifiability: one state has one realized action/noise label. A flexible state-dependent intercept and derivatives can explain that realization in multiple ways. Hundreds of fits of the same realization do not create independent action comparisons. Historical replay supplies more neighboring states, actions and behavior-distribution coordinates, while anchoring representations on older experience.

The approximation also omits cross-action second-order and higher-order terms. Their contribution is unmeasured. Prioritized Huber regression changes the population projection relative to unweighted squared-error Hermite moments, so Gaussian orthogonality alone does not prove that the learned center is the exact Gaussian expectation.

The backup queries sigma=0.2 as an input coordinate, even when collection concentrates closer to sigma=0.1. There is no architectural constraint requiring F(s,mu,sigma,mu) to equal E[Q(s,clip(mu+sigma*eps))]. Unlike ordinary action smoothing, this can extrapolate in a distribution coordinate without evaluating the underlying action function. Repeated target copies can feed that error back into a fixed fresh dataset.

SALE adds another moving coordinate system: encoder optimization specializes to the fresh dataset; fixed-encoder swaps alter both critic features and actor outputs. Replay can resist this forgetting, so its benefit cannot be attributed solely to TD targets.

Experiments and predictions

  • v10 cadence control: 32 steps per lane, 32 critic updates and 16 actor updates per 512 transitions. This jointly reduces the collection delay and optimizer/data ratio, preserving architecture, losses, exploration transition budget and evaluation spacing. Prediction: reduced policy displacement; recovery would support overoptimization as the main failure. It was stopped near 2M because return remained around 6k. Cadence alone did not recover replay performance.
  • v11 physical action-Q: learn Q(s,a); compute actor value and continuation values with eight antithetic clipped Gaussian action samples. Mean/std derivatives must now belong to one action function. This removes the free distribution-coordinate inconsistency, but does not guarantee accurate action gradients from finite data.
  • v11 frozen behavior targets: compute all labels once before fitting, using the actual behavior distribution and the target critic. Terminal states stop bootstrapping; timeouts bootstrap their final observations and stop trace recursion. Compare lambda=0.95 with lambda=0 under the same frozen-target procedure. This isolates trace length; comparison to v10 also changes critic architecture, smoothing, policy evaluation and label freezing.

At gamma=0.99, lambda=0.95 retains about 0.832 of a constant continuation-value bias in the infinite-horizon limit (roughly 0.855 at the first row of a 32-step rollout). It is not a cure for persistent absolute value bias. Its rationale is observed reward/action credit and eliminating within-fit label feedback, with increased target variance as the tradeoff.

Bootstrap depth changes with update count

TD7 initializes continuation-value clipping at zero and expands it from observed TD labels at hard target copies. With positive rewards bounded by R, the clipping maximum after k copies has upper envelope 100 × R × (1 − 0.99^k). This is an idealized bound, not a prediction: the target critic must approach the current range to expand it, and policy rewards change during training.

Original v9 makes about 4,000 copies per million transitions; the short-cadence controls make 250; large-rollout four-pass fitting makes only 62.5. Even the idealized envelope reaches only 47% of its asymptotic bound after 1M in the latter case, versus 92% for the short control. Lower update counts therefore reduce fitting and target-feedback depth together. Absolute Q comparisons must also account for actual policy reward: Q near episode return/10 is plausible at gamma=.99 with roughly stationary rewards and 1,000-step episodes.

Frozen lambda labels change this propagation mechanism. For a segment of L uninterrupted transitions with rewards bounded above by R and continuation predictions bounded above by H, its label is bounded by a_L × R + b_L × H, where a_L = (1 − (gamma × lambda)^L)/(1 − gamma × lambda) and b_L = 1 − (1 − gamma) × a_L. At lambda=.95, b_32 is approximately .855 and b_1024 approximately .832, rather than the one-step .99. This motivates the large-batch lambda run: observed reward credit can expand the label range faster without more optimizer passes. It remains an idealized label bound; terminal masks, critic underfitting, changing rewards and the frozen-per-rollout target schedule determine actual propagation.

Likelihood-gradient actor control

v12 retains physical action-Q and frozen lambda targets, but uses the integrated action-Q as an action-independent baseline for normalized frozen advantages. The actor takes a clipped PPO Gaussian-likelihood update on raw pre-actions, sampling states uniformly instead of from critic priorities. This is a behavioral diagnostic, not a claimed novel algorithm. It changes gradient source, state weighting, advantage scale, surrogate clipping and actor gradient clipping together.

How the next decision is made

Before actor fitting, v11 compares its physical-Q pathwise gradient with a fresh score-function estimate using frozen returns and an action-independent state baseline. The estimate uses raw pre-actions, whose Gaussian likelihood remains valid when execution clips actions. Entropy is excluded; diagnostic RNG is isolated. The cosine is a noisy diagnostic, not an oracle: continuation values are approximate and each state has one noise sample.

In the v11 lambda run after 1M, mean cosine is about 0.04 and path/score norm ratio about 0.03. These numbers are compatible with a correct path gradient plus large orthogonal score noise: cosine would approximately equal the norm ratio. Per-snapshot directional projections average about 1.5 but vary widely in sign. There is no established wrong-gradient result. v12 adds environment-block projections and split-score reliability to distinguish estimator noise from directional bias.

Reliably negative directional projections plus poor learning favor an on-policy likelihood-gradient actor or a compatible, cross-fitted control variate over unconstrained fitted derivatives. Good initial alignment followed by large measured drift favors a behavior-relative policy trust budget. Good alignment and controlled drift with poor learning shifts attention toward target bias, entropy allocation and credit length.

v10 also records critic loss/Hermite fit before and after learning and the difference between its latent center value and an antithetic average of physical point queries. Its default has no held-out lanes, so these are fitting diagnostics. The optional whole-lane holdout is available, but no held-out generalization conclusion is claimed here.

Cancelled baseline pair and the next mechanism

Jobs 12004 and 12005 were cancelled before starting. The user challenged repeating fresh-versus-replay baselines; neither will be resubmitted. Replay benefit is already established. The v15 probes remain available for new latent-Q interventions, but report drift rather than correctness.

The next decision concerns unsupported distribution coordinates in the TD backup. The completed four-pass run has center-to-point convolution MAE approximately 0.084 near 2M transitions, with fitted Q approximately 186. This is a small discrepancy at its behavior queries. The backup instead uses sigma=0.2, which these logged comparisons did not separately evaluate. Existing evidence therefore does not yet justify replacing the backup.

An independently reviewed checkpoint audit will compare latent centers and their full mean/std derivatives with two independent point-convolution panels on freshly collected states, separately at behavior sigma, 0.1 and 0.2. Panel disagreement measures reference sampling uncertainty. This is internal consistency evidence, not environmental accuracy: both sides use the same learned critic.

Discriminating prediction: if unsupported backup coordinates drive recursive error, sigma=0.2 discrepancies should exceed behavior-coordinate discrepancies and independent-panel noise. A backup-only intervention would query the existing latent critic at physical points, average each twin across clipped Gaussian actions, then take the twin minimum and apply the existing bounds. Averaging action-wise twin minima would also alter pessimism and is excluded. Latent mean/std conditioning, Hermite supervision and latent actor would remain intact.

If target-coordinate discrepancy is small, that intervention loses priority. Reliable disagreement between center derivatives and point-convolution derivatives would instead motivate a distribution-consistency intervention. Agreement on both would shift priority toward critic feature replacement.
